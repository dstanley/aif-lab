## Uncertain points (left out or handled carefully)

- `SUSEConnect -list-extensions` uses a single dash on the SLES 15 SP7 page, but the Micro page documents `--list-extensions`. The record quotes the SLES page exactly and notes the spelling. A reviewer may prefer to verify it against `man 8 SUSEConnect`.
- The SLES 16 registration CLI was not documented on the pages I read. Only Agama registration and "deregister with SUSEConnect and register again" are described. The SUSEConnect command examples are therefore labelled SLES 15 SP7. The module/extension examples are also SLES 15 only, because SLES 16 removes modules.
- The Container Guide contradicts itself on derived images. §22.2 says "A SLE subscription is required for building derived containers", while §22.4 says no subscription is needed for content from BCIs or SLE_BCI. I used only the clear statement that you do not need a subscription to run or redistribute BCIs, plus §22.4.
- The Container Guide says BCI-Base is the largest general-purpose BCI, but its size table shows BCI-Init as larger (167 MB vs 135 MB). I avoided calling either one "the largest".
- The SUSE AI 1.0 guide calls for SLES 15 SP6 / SUSE Linux Micro 6.1 nodes (and says "SUSE Linux Enterprise Micro 6.1" in the driver section). SUSE AI Factory calls for SLES 15.7+. I attributed each requirement to its own product. I skipped the Micro GPU-driver commands, because that page mixes product names and contains a stray "transactional update #" prompt on an SLES line.
- The SLES 16 Cockpit page has typos (`firewall-cmd –-reload`, `sudo sudo zypper`). Only the clean commands (`zypper in -t pattern cockpit`, `systemctl enable --now cockpit.socket`) are used.
- The GPU Operator manifest version in the SUSE AI doc is v25.3.4, but its note talks about v25.10.x and CDI. Neither version number is used in the records.
- Application Collection quota numbers (users and pulls per subscription) were left out. They change, and they sit close to commercial terms.
- There are no pricing or entitlement claims beyond what the docs say: "SUSE AI is a separate entitlement from SUSE Rancher Prime", and "modules are included in the subscription, extensions need extra registration codes that must be purchased".
- Eval and train overlap: the eval items ask different questions, but a few rely on facts the training set touches indirectly. For example, Agama/Cockpit are mentioned in train, and eval asks what replaces YaST for 1:1 management. Train also covers user vs service accounts, which the eval's CI-credential question draws on. Topics held out of train entirely: `zypper ps`, `--cve` patching, the transactional-update timer, `refresh -fdb`, the SLES 16 activation package, BusyBox/GPLv3, FIPS images, 429 rate limits, toolbox, /tmp tmpfs, the container-suseconnect warning, UEFI-only Micro, Ollama 0.x to 1.x, and firewall egress hosts.
